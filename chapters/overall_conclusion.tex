\label{chap:overall_conclusion}

The main objective of this thesis was to develop Intelligent Decision Support System (IDSS) for the manufacturing industry, aimed at assisting operators in making decisions concerning the appropriate configuration of machinery for specific production processes.

The work carried out with the proposed thesis demonstrated that addressing complex real-world industrial problems can benefit from the integration of different and complementary techniques. Rather than relying on a single methodology, the proposed approaches combine methods with different characteristics to address the specific challenges posed by each problem.

In the first part of the thesis, the solution to the \emph{Fixture Layout Optimization} problem was achieved by integrating exact optimization techniques with local search algorithms. In the second part, the \emph{Heating Process Optimization} problem was addressed using neural network models trained on data generated by a physics simulator, whose predictions were subsequently refined through independent iterations of the Adam optimizer.

Beyond the methodological contributions, the work carried out in this thesis produced results of direct relevance to the industrial partner and contributed to bringing academic research closer to industry. The proposed approaches introduced techniques that had not previously been adopted within the company and explored optimization strategies that had not yet been considered for the addressed production processes.

The value of this thesis lies particularly in this connection between research and industrial practice, as the challenges encountered in real manufacturing processes directly motivated the research questions and the development of the proposed solutions, following what can be described as an industry-inspired research.
